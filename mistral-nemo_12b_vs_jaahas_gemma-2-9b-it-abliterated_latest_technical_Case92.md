**Forensic Analysis of Architectural, Symbolic, and Astronomical Parallels Across Ancient Structures**

**1. Architectural Parallels**

- **Giza, Teotihuacan, Pyramid of the Sun:**
  - *Alignment:* All three pyramids are aligned within 1° of true north.
  - *Scale:* Giza's Great Pyramid has a base area of 5.3 hectares and a volume of 2.6 million cubic meters. Teotihuacan's Pyramid of the Sun has a base area of 1.3 hectares and a volume of 1.2 million cubic meters. The Pyramid of the Sun at Teotihuacan is taller (230m vs. 146m) but has a smaller base area than the Great Pyramid.
  - *Construction:* Both use large stone blocks (Giza's average 2.5 tons, Teotihuacan's up to 80 tons), requiring significant quarrying, transportation, and lifting capabilities.

- **Inca Temples, Mayan Step Pyramids, Angkor Wat:**
  - *Scale:* Machu Picchu's Intihuatana stone weighs 50 tons. Mayan step pyramids like El Castillo at Chichen Itza have a base area of 0.5 hectares and a volume of 0.9 million cubic meters. Angkor Wat's temple complex covers 1.6 square kilometers.
  - *Construction:* Inca temples used local stones, while Mayan and Khmer structures used sandstone and laterite, respectively, requiring extensive quarrying and transportation.

**2. Symbolic Parallels**

- All structures exhibit:
  - *Astronomical alignments:* Solstice and equinox alignments, suggesting advanced knowledge of celestial events.
  - *Water features:* Moats, fountains, or water channels, indicating understanding of water management.
  - *Sacred geometry:* Use of circles, squares, and other geometric shapes, reflecting cosmological beliefs.

**3. Astronomical Parallels**

- **Giza and Teotihuacan:**
  - Both have solstice alignments with their main entrances.
  - Giza's Great Pyramid is aligned with the polar star at the time of its construction (c. 2560 BCE), while Teotihuacan's Pyramid of the Sun is aligned with the equinox sunrise.

- **Other structures:**
  - Mayan step pyramids have solstice and equinox alignments.
  - Angkor Wat's central tower aligns with the cardinal directions, and its moat reflects the shape of the universe in Hindu cosmology.

**4. Resource and Technological Constraints**

- *Resource requirements:* Massive stone blocks required extensive quarrying, transportation, and lifting technologies. Labor force estimates range from tens of thousands (Giza) to hundreds of thousands (Teotihuacan).
- *Technological constraints:* Ramps, levers, and possibly pulley systems were used for construction. The use of large stones implies advanced lifting technologies.

**5. Timeline Constraints**

- *Dating methods:* Radiocarbon dating places the Great Pyramid at c. 2560 BCE, Teotihuacan's Pyramid of the Sun at c. 100-450 CE, Machu Picchu at c. 1450 CE, Mayan step pyramids at c. 1000 BCE - 1600 CE, and Angkor Wat at c. 1113 CE.
- *Limitations:* Lack of written records makes precise dating difficult. Stylistic and technological similarities may suggest shared knowledge or diffusion but do not prove a common cataclysmic event.

**Implications**

The shared architectural, symbolic, and astronomical features suggest a level of cultural exchange or diffusion. However, without written records, it's challenging to prove a shared memory of a cataclysmic event predating recorded history. The structures' scale and technological constraints imply advanced societies with significant resources and organizational capabilities.
