The architectural, symbolic, and astronomical parallels among the pyramids at Giza, Teotihuacan, and the Pyramid of the Sun, Inca temples, Mayan step pyramids, and Angkor Wat suggest a shared pattern of design rather than a direct memory of a cataclysmic event. Analyzing these features through a systems-engineering lens reveals several key insights.

**Architectural Parallels:**

1. **Shape and Structure:** The most prominent architectural feature is the pyramid shape, which is a stable and efficient structure for distributing weight. The slope angles of these pyramids vary but are generally between 40° and 50°, which is an optimal angle for structural stability against wind forces and seismic activity. For instance, the Great Pyramid of Giza has an average slope of approximately 51.84°.

2. **Step Pyramids:** The step pyramids, such as those found in the Mayan sites, are a variant of the pyramid shape that allows for easier construction and stability on uneven terrain. The step design also serves to reduce the force of impact from earthquakes.

3. **Material Usage:** Many of these structures were built using local materials, such as limestone for the Giza pyramids and andesite for the Teotihuacan pyramids. This choice of materials was likely driven by availability and ease of transportation.

**Symbolic Parallels:**

1. **Cardinal Directions:** Many of these structures are aligned with cardinal directions, suggesting a shared symbolic system. For example, the Pyramid of the Sun at Teotihuacan is aligned with the sunrise during the spring and autumn equinoxes.

2. **Celestial Alignments:** There are astronomical alignments in these structures, such as the alignment of the pyramids at Giza with the stars in Orion's Belt. However, the precision of these alignments varies, and their significance is not universally agreed upon.

**Astronomical Analysis:**

1. **Precision:** While some alignments are precise, such as the Pyramid of the Sun's alignment with the sunrise, others are less precise. The alignment of the Giza pyramids with the constellation of Orion is notable but not exact.

2. **Significance:** The significance of these alignments is often tied to the local mythology and astronomical knowledge of the time. For example, the alignment of Angkor Wat with the sunrise and sunset points reflects the Khmer Empire's understanding of astronomy.

**Conclusion:**

The architectural, symbolic, and astronomical parallels among these structures suggest a shared pattern of design influenced by local resources, environmental conditions, and cultural knowledge. While there is no direct evidence of a shared memory of a cataclysmic event, the precision and significance of these alignments and designs imply a sophisticated level of knowledge and planning among ancient civilizations. This knowledge may have been acquired through trade, migration, or a common ancestral heritage, but without direct historical records, these theories remain speculative.
