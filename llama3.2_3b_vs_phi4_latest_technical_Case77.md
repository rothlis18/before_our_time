**Architectural, Symbolic, and Astronomical Parallels**

The three pyramids (Giza, Teotihuacan, and the Pyramid of the Sun), Inca temples, Mayan step pyramids, and Angkor Wat exhibit striking architectural, symbolic, and astronomical parallels. These similarities can be attributed to the shared knowledge and technological heritage of ancient civilizations.

**Material Properties and Construction Techniques**

Comparative analysis of material properties and construction techniques reveals a consistent emphasis on limestone, granite, and sandstone. The use of these materials suggests a shared understanding of geological properties and resource allocation. The pyramids' construction, for instance, required an enormous amount of limestone, which was often quarried from nearby sites. This indicates a high degree of organization and logistical planning, characteristic of a shared technological heritage.

**Astronomical Alignments**

The pyramids' alignments with celestial bodies, such as the rising and setting of the sun, demonstrate a sophisticated understanding of astronomy. The Pyramid of the Sun, for example, is aligned with the winter solstice sunrise, while the Pyramid of Kukulkan at Chichen Itza is aligned with the descent of the feathered serpent deity. These alignments suggest a shared understanding of astronomical phenomena and a common cultural context.

**Symbolic Significance**

The use of similar symbolic motifs, such as the ankh and the feathered serpent, across different civilizations indicates a shared cultural heritage. These symbols often represented life, fertility, and spiritual renewal, suggesting a common understanding of human experience and the natural world.

**Resource Allocation and Economic Constraints**

The construction of these massive structures required significant resources, including labor, materials, and infrastructure. The pyramids' construction, for instance, required an enormous amount of limestone, which was often quarried from nearby sites. This indicates a high degree of organization and logistical planning, characteristic of a shared technological heritage.

**Systemic Realism**

The construction of these structures can be seen as a manifestation of systemic realism, where the economic, social, and technological constraints of each civilization influenced the design and construction of the pyramids. The pyramids' construction, for example, required a high degree of organization and logistical planning, which was only possible through the development of sophisticated administrative systems.

**Conclusion**

The architectural, symbolic, and astronomical parallels among the three pyramids, Inca temples, Mayan step pyramids, and Angkor Wat suggest a shared memory of a cataclysmic event and an unspoken epoch of human civilization predating recorded history. The similarities in material properties, construction techniques, astronomical alignments, symbolic significance, and resource allocation indicate a shared technological heritage and a common cultural context. This shared knowledge and technological heritage can be seen as a manifestation of systemic realism, where the economic, social, and technological constraints of each civilization influenced the design and construction of the pyramids.
